How NE India Pulse works

This app answers one question — “what is North East India talking and feeling about today?” — by reading the world’s news, focusing on the eight NE states, and summarising the themes, mood, people/organisations and volume of coverage. It refreshes with new data every 15 minutes.

How we measure “Mood” (tone)

Every article GDELT processes gets a tone score: roughly positive words − negative words as a percentage of the text. It typically lands between about −10 and +10. We take the average tone of all NE-India articles in the window and place it on this scale:

We also show the split of how many articles were positive, neutral or negative — so a calm average that hides a 40%-negative day is still visible.

What this is — and isn’t

NE India Pulse measures the news-media conversation, not public opinion. It’s a useful, fast, neutral read on what the press is reporting and the tone of that reporting — but it is not a poll of what residents think.
  • Media, not minds. No social media, messaging or surveys — just published news.
  • Language skew. Coverage leans to English/Hindi online news; Assamese, Meitei and Bodo vernacular outlets are largely uncaptured.
  • Geocoding is noisy. Smaller states (Arunachal, Mizoram) show thin signal; national-dateline stories can be mis-placed.
  • Best read over hours, not minutes. A single 15-minute slice is tiny, so we aggregate a window and (soon) compare against a baseline.
